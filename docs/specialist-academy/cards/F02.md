# F02 — Validation first

## Flash (quiz)

**Front:** Why send validation to the collector before relying only on bureau dispute letters for the same debt?

**Back:** Validation (FDCPA §809 educational framing) demands verification from the collector and can pause improper collection while the file is built.

---

## Teach this like a lesson

### Plain explanation

The collector track and the bureau track are related but not identical. Validation speaks to the **collector’s duty to verify** before pressuring payment. Bureau disputes speak to **accuracy on the credit report**.

### Why it matters

Without validation on the timeline, a partner can be sued or harassed while we mail generic “delete” letters to Experian. That looks disorganized to courts and regulators.

### Step-by-step

1. Date the first collector letter.
2. Mail validation (certified).
3. Log in Debt Center.
4. Screenshot tradeline for later Round 1.
5. Align bureau language with validation facts.

### Example — Marie, Haitian desk

Collector letter arrives Tuesday. Thursday validation mails. Partner hears in Kreyòl: “Nou mande prèv dèt la anvan nou atake biwo a.” English letter attached in portal.

### Common mistakes

- Promising the tradeline “must” disappear after validation.
- Skipping validation because Round 1 templates are ready.

### Checklist

- [ ] Validation mailed with proof
- [ ] No contradictory bureau narrative
