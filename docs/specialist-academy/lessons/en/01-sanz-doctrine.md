# Sanz doctrine — the Finely restore sequence

Finely Cred is not a “delete everything” shop. We restore files with **discipline** so partners can move toward **wealth paths**—funding readiness, business credit, and calm household finances—without fantasy score promises. This lesson is the spine every specialist memorizes before touching a live file.

## Why this matters

Bureaus, collectors, and courts each speak a different language. When specialists skip steps, partners get **contradictory letters**, missed **court dates**, or **cookie-cutter dispute spam** that wastes months. The Sanz doctrine orders work so the file stays **litigation-ready** and honest.

## The nine pillars (teach in order)

1. **Debt-first** when collection or summons risk exists.
2. **Validation first** on collector debt (FDCPA §809 educational framing)—before bureau-only campaigns and before collection pressure escalates without a paper trail.
3. **Summons literacy**—calendars, affidavits awareness, never ignore court; counsel when needed.
4. **Bureau restore rounds**—FCRA factual disputes with exhibits.
5. **Complaints ladder** after failed rounds when warranted—not revenge spam.
6. **Litigation-ready documentation** every step.
7. **OCR / template variation**—no repetitive walls of identical text.
8. **Screenshot evidence** on the actual reported account.
9. **Round-stage awareness**—prior companies, prior letters, never blind Round 1 restarts.

## Step-by-step: how you apply it on day one

1. Run **SOP-14** intake: prior vendor, rounds mailed, validation/summons history.
2. Run **SOP-08** triage: classify `debt_first`, `parallel_with_caution`, or `bureau_only`.
3. If `debt_first`, open **Debt & Summons Center** and schedule **SOP-09** validation before batch bureau work.
4. Upload report, capture **SOP-13** evidence per account.
5. Begin **BUILD** education in parallel (SOP-15 § B)—payment discipline and mix literacy, not new card spam during court clocks.
6. When gates clear, **SOP-11** Round 1 with varied templates.
7. Track responses; Round 2 tightens facts; complaints only per **SOP-12**.
8. **SOP-15** before any Nora language.

## Example — Marie (Haitian desk, Miami)

Marie sees a **Fresh Collections LLC** letter and an Experian tradeline updated last week. Her cousin wants “Round 1 delete everything tonight.”

**Correct Finely sequence:** Sit together in **Kreyòl**; explain validation window in plain language. Draft **validation** to Fresh Collections (English letter). Screenshot the **Experian tradeline** for later bureau work. Create tasks: mail validation certified; follow-up day 30. **Pause** mass deletion language until validation track is started. Bureau dispute on inaccurate fields can run later without contradicting validation facts.

> **Example — script (Kreyòl layer):** “Nou pa ignore kolektè a. Nou voye lèt validasyon an Angle paske biwo yo li Angle. Nou kenbe kopi tout bagay nan portal la.”

## Example — James (prior credit company)

James says “Credit Pro already sent Round 2 in March.” Upload his PDFs. Inventory shows Round 2 to EQ/EX for two charge-offs.

**Correct Finely sequence:** Do **not** re-mail Round 1 boilerplate. Open Round 2 follow-up or Round 3 with **new structure** (OCR SOP) and exhibits tied to bureau responses. Note prior vendor in file narrative.

> **Mistake — do not:** Restart Round 1 because Finely templates “look nicer.” You will confuse the furnisher timeline.

## Common mistakes specialists make

- Jumping to CFPB before mailing disciplined disputes.
- Promising “Nora will approve you after this round.”
- Identical validation letters across ten accounts (OCR pattern risk).
- Screenshot of credit **score only** without the tradeline body.
