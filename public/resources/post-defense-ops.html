PHASE 2: POST-DEFENSE

The Shift in Power

The moment you finish reading your opening script, the courtroom dynamics permanently change. You have admitted the truth of your past, but demanded legal proof of their present authority. The burden is now entirely on Midland.

What Happens Next

The Magistrate will turn to the opposing attorney. The spotlight is no longer on you; it is on their paperwork.

The Magistrate's Likely Response:

"Counsel, what do you have to show me? Have you two spoken yet? Please step out into the hallway and see if you can work this out."

Magistrates heavily prefer not to rule if they do not have to. They will usually order you into the hallway to attempt mediation. This is completely normal. Do not panic.

The Hallway Protocol

When you step outside the courtroom, the opposing attorney will try to pressure you one-on-one. They want you to fold so they don't have to face the judge again. Hold the line.

Your Posture

Be incredibly polite, calm, and firm. You are a professional conducting business. Let them do most of the talking.

The Hallway Script

"I am perfectly willing to resolve this today, but you still haven't shown me the legal proof that you own my specific account. Without that, I'm not comfortable signing an agreement. What exactly are you offering?"

The Document Ambush

Whether in the hallway or back in front of the judge, the attorney may suddenly produce a stack of papers—usually a generic "Bill of Sale" and some old Citibank statements. This is designed to intimidate you.

How to Disarm Their Paperwork

Take the documents. Look at them carefully. Do not act surprised. You are looking for one specific flaw: The generic bulk sale.

Ask The Attorney / The Judge:

"Your Honor, this Bill of Sale mentions a 'Portfolio of Accounts,' but it does not actually name me or my exact account. Where is the specific row or data field that connects me to this bulk sale?"

Follow Up Firmly:

"I am not asking the Court to ignore these documents. I am just pointing out that they do not legally connect this exact plaintiff to my exact account."

Mastering the 30-Day Extension

Our ultimate goal is a Dismissal. However, if the plaintiff lacks proof on Day 1, the judge will likely grant a Continuance (extending the case for about 30 days). Do not view this as a loss. This is a massive tactical victory.

Why 30 Days? The Legal Reality

Under Small Claims Trial Rules, plaintiffs are strictly required to be fully prepared with their evidence on Day 1. When you invoke your rights under UCC 9-406 to demand proof of the exact account assignment, and they don't have it, they are legally unprepared.

The judge isn't giving them 30 days because it's a special law; the judge is giving them a standard scheduling grace period because their case is defective today.

Why This Hurts Them

Midland buys debts in bulk and relies on cheap, automated software. Forcing them to manually locate, extract, and legally verify your specific account-level data from a massive corporate portfolio costs them severe time and money. Often, they simply cannot find it.

If The Judge Grants Them More Time, Say This:

"Your Honor, I object to a continuance since they are unprepared for trial today. However, if the Court grants them more time, I ask that they be strictly required to produce the account-level assignment and a mathematical accounting of this specific balance, not just a generic bill of sale."

Guided Operations

Your behavior in the courtroom and the hallway will be heavily scrutinized. Maintain absolute discipline.

DO THIS

  • ✔ Speak clearly and maintain eye contact with the Magistrate.
  • ✔ Ask to physically hold and read any document they try to use against you.
  • ✔ Take a breath before answering questions.
  • ✔ Remember you can always end hallway mediation by saying, "Let's go back before the judge."

DO NOT DO THIS

  • ✘ Do not get angry, raise your voice, or argue with the opposing attorney.
  • ✘ Do not say "I don't owe you anything." (It sounds defensive, not factual).
  • ✘ Do not sign a consent judgment just to go home early.
  • ✘ Do not agree to "convenience" payment plans if they cannot prove ownership.

Final Exit Operations

No matter the outcome, you leave the courtroom as a professional. Here is how to handle the end of the proceeding.

If Dismissed:

Say, "Thank you, Your Honor." Collect your papers, walk out calmly, and immediately report the victory to the Finely Cred team.

If Granted a 30-Day Continuance:

Write down the new court date immediately. Ensure you have stated your objection on the record, thank the judge, and leave. The pressure is now entirely on them to find the data.

If Judgment is Entered Against You:

Sometimes judges rule incorrectly despite the law. If they enter a judgment, do not argue. Say "Thank you, Your Honor," and leave. Finely Cred will pivot immediately to post-judgment strategy.

You are prepared. You control the narrative.
