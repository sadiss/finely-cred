The issue is not whether a Citibank account once existed. The issue is whether this named plaintiff can prove ownership, authority, and accurate accounting today.

The Top 2% Mindset

Midland expects consumers to panic and deny everything. You should do the opposite. Admit only the truth, but concede nothing else. Credibility is your ultimate weapon.

The Controlling Sentence

"Yes, I did have a Citibank account in the past. But I cannot confirm if the paperwork they are showing me today is for the exact account I had, nor do I admit that this specific company owns it."

1. Stay Calm

Speak strictly to the Magistrate, not the opposing attorney.

2. Control The Pace

Pause for two full seconds before answering any question.

3. Absolute Truth

Do not guess. Say "I do not know" when that is the honest truth.

4. Force The Proof

Make Midland legally prove their ownership, transfer chain, and amount.

The Five-Gate Strategy

Midland must cross every single gate. You do not need to prove a negative; you win if the plaintiff fails to prove a necessary link.

01

Named Plaintiff Authority

Is the exact entity on the complaint legally entitled to sue, or is it only a servicer/collector? The exact plaintiff must be the owner or have written authority.

02

The Transfer Chain

What document transferred this account? Does the bill of sale specifically name you or your account, or is it just a generic bulk portfolio?

03

Account-Level Match

Where is the exact row, record, or data field that identifies your specific account? Does it show the true balance at the time of transfer?

04

Amount Legality

Are the balance, interest, fees, costs, and post-charge-off additions authorized by contract and mathematically traced?

05

Witness Reliability

Did the opposing witness actually work for Citi? Did they create the records or participate in the sale? Or are they merely reading a computer screen?

Courtroom Execution Scripts

Memorize the text in bold. Speak clearly, politely, and directly to the Magistrate.

Phase 1: The Opening

"Yes, Your Honor, I did have a Citibank account in the past. But I cannot confirm if the paperwork they are showing me today is for the exact account I had, nor do I admit that this specific company owns it. Until they can prove that with actual records I can verify, I respectfully ask them to prove their case."

Phase 2: If They Produce Documents

Ask the Plaintiff:

  • "Does the bill of sale actually name me or my exact account?"
  • "Where is the row, record or data field that connects me to this bulk sale?"

"I am not asking the Court to ignore documents. I am asking what each document proves, what it does not prove, and whether it legally connects this exact plaintiff to this exact receivable and amount."

Phase 3: Challenging the Witness

"I ask the Court to give limited weight to any conclusion unless the account-specific record and a witness capable of explaining it are before the Court."

Statutory Armor & Precedent

You do not need to read these laws aloud. This is the legal machinery operating behind your script.

The Law

Mass. Rule 17 (Real Party)

Requires an action to be prosecuted in the name of the real party in interest. This forces them to prove which exact entity owns the receivable.

UCC 9-406 (Assignment)

Reasonable proof of assignment may be requested. A generic bill of sale is not enough without the individual account connection.

FDCPA & Mass. 93A Leverage

Using a wrong balance, adding unauthorized fees, or misrepresenting legal status can trigger FDCPA and Massachusetts consumer-protection pressure.

Case Precedent

Mitchell-James Precedent

Admission of using an unspecified original account did not concede account-specific debt-buyer ownership.

Biehl Precedent

A bill of sale and statements were not enough where the account-level sale file link was missing.

Coleman Precedent

A bare affidavit claiming ownership without assignment documents was rejected.

What to bring to court and how to handle their attempts to settle.

The Settlement Trap

Do not accidentally legitimize their lawsuit. If they cannot prove ownership and exact accounting, they have no legal ground to demand money.

  • Do not sign a consent judgment.
  • Do not agree to "convenience" payments.
  • Do not admit liability just to leave early.
  • The goal is a Dismissal, or forcing a Continuance so they have to prove it.
"Since you cannot legally prove ownership of this specific account, I am not open to a settlement. I am requesting a full dismissal of this case."
