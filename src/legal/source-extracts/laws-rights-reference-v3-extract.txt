MAP  How to Use the Law Stack  Each law must connect to a fact, a document gap, and a remedy.  02  1   Procedure   Does the court filing comply with small-claims and assigned-debt  requirements?  2   Capacity   Is the named plaintiff the real party in interest or otherwise  authorized to sue?  3   Assignment   Did rights actually transfer to plaintiff, and did the documents  identify this exact account?  4   Assignee defenses   Did original-transaction claims, payment issues or fee defects travel  with the receivable?  5   Collection law   Did the collector misstate amount, legal status, authority, fees or  credit reporting?  6   Remedy   Dismissal, judgment for defendant, continuance, reduction,  complaint leverage, or settlement leverage.  Rule of discipline  Do not stack statutes like decoration. Pick the two or three that fit the actual documents. A precise  factual attack beats a pile of legal words.  Educational self-help material. MASSACHUSETTS COURT RULES  Small Claims: The Foundation  These rules shape what happens at the hearing and what must be checked first.  03  Burden of proof  Plaintiff must prove the claim by a preponderance of the evidence. If proof is evenly balanced,  plaintiff should not win.  Assigned-debt filing data  For assigned debt, the statement of claim must include original creditor, last four account digits, and  amount/date of last payment if any.  Evidence is relaxed, not meaningless  Small-claims evidence rules allow flexibility. The better argument is weight, reliability, missing links  and account-specific proof.  Answer is useful, even if optional  A written answer can map the disputed issues and prevent nervous improvisation. It should not read  like an internet manifesto.  Court use  Plain phrasing  “I am not asking the Court to reject every document. I am asking the Court to weigh whether these  documents reliably prove ownership, authority and amount.”  Educational self-help material. Verify facts, court dates, and filings before use. No outcome is guaranteed.

REAL PARTY  Rule 17 and Entity Authority  This is the LLC / affiliate / servicer problem - use it carefully.  04  Massachusetts Rule 17 requires an action to be prosecuted by the real party in interest, while  allowing certain authorized parties and written assignees of non-negotiable claims to sue in their  own names.  1   Exact name   Match plaintiff name to the bill of sale, affidavits, license, and  account records.  2   Owner or servicer?   Midland affiliate structures matter: owner, servicer and collector are  not the same role.  3   Authority document   If the named plaintiff is not the owner, ask for servicing agreement,  agency authority, POA or assignment.  4   Foreign entity status   Registration or capacity issues can matter, but defects may be  curable. Verify before using.  Court-safe version  “I am asking whether this particular legal entity is the real party in interest or otherwise  authorized to sue in its own name.”  Do not overclaim  Do not argue that “LLCs cannot sue.” They can. The issue is whether this entity owns or is  authorized to enforce this receivable.  Educational self-help material. Verify facts, court dates, and filings before use. No outcome is guaranteed.

UCC ARTICLE 9  Assignment, Proof and Assignee  Defenses Article 9 is stronger than Article 3 for ordinary sold credit-card receivables.  05  UCC 9-404 - defenses travel  Assignee rights can be subject to the original agreement and defenses or claims in recoupment  arising from the transaction. Use this for payment, credit, fee, billing and contract-term defects.  UCC 9-406 - proof of assignment  An account debtor may request reasonable proof of assignment. Useful for identifying who is entitled  to payment; not an automatic debt eraser.  UCC 9-203 / 9-607 - rights and enforcement authority  Use only with the sale/security documents. Ask whether rights attached, what collateral was  described, and who has enforcement authority.  Best use in court  Do not say “UCC voids it.” Say: “Article 9 requires us to identify the rights transferred, the entity  entitled to enforce them, and any defenses or recoupment claims that survived assignment.”  Question  “What exact rights were assigned, to whom, on what date, under what document, and subject to what defenses or  conditions?”  Educational self-help material. Verify facts, court dates, and filings before use. No outcome is guaranteed.

CONSUMER PROTECTION  FDCPA, 940 CMR 7 and Chapter 93A  These can turn a defensive file into pressure if the facts support it.  06  1   False amount/status   FDCPA 1692e and 940 CMR 7.07 target false or misleading  statements about amount or legal status.  2   Unauthorized charges   FDCPA 1692f and 940 CMR 7.07 attack interest, fees, charges or  expenses not authorized by agreement or law.  3   Wrong business name   Using a name other than the true creditor/debt collector name may  create issues if misleading.  4   Dispute reporting   After dispute, failure to communicate a debt as disputed can be a  problem under debt-collection rules.  5   Massachusetts 93A   Unfair or deceptive practices can create remedies, but require facts,  injury and proper procedure.  Use this as leverage  If documents show the wrong entity, wrong amount, unauthorized add-ons, misleading ownership,  or contradictory reporting, the file is no longer only about defending a $1,094 claim. It becomes a  compliance problem.  Educational self-help material. Verify facts, court dates, and filings before use. No outcome is guaranteed.

LICENSING  Collector, Buyer, Attorney: Who Needs  What? Licensing is fact-specific. It can create pressure but does not automatically erase principal.  07  Massachusetts guidance says debt collectors and debt buyers that directly collect consumer debt  need appropriate licensing, while passive debt buyers using licensed collectors or Massachusetts  attorneys may be treated differently. Attorneys collecting debt are subject to other rules, including  FDCPA and professional conduct obligations.  1   Identify each actor   Owner, servicer, collector, attorney, data provider, purchaser.  2   Check timing   Was the relevant actor licensed/registered when collection activity  occurred?  3   Attorney exception   A Massachusetts attorney may not need a DOB debt-collector  license, but still faces FDCPA/professional rules.  4   Court use   Use licensing to question authority, regulatory compliance, and  misleading collection conduct.  Question  “Which entity directly collected this debt, what role did it play, and what license, registration,  attorney authority, or exemption applies?”  Educational self-help material. Verify facts, court dates, and filings before use. No outcome is guaranteed.

ARBITRATION AND CITI TERMS  The Agreement Can Cut Both Ways  Do not assume. Pull the exact historical Citi card agreement.  08  The exact Citi agreement matters. It can define interest, fees, arbitration, governing law, assignment  rights, account-stated language, payment application, default, and collection costs. The CFPB  agreement database helps locate general Citi agreements, but the account-specific version still  matters.  1   Product match   Same Citi product, opening period, governing law and  amendments.  2   Arbitration   Check who can elect, when, forum, fees, delegation, and small-  claims carve-out.  3   Fees and interest   No agreement term, no lawful add-on. Tie all charges to specific  language.  4   Assignment clause   Does the agreement allow assignment? What defenses survive?  What notice is required?  Court-safe version  “Before accepting the amount or terms, I ask that plaintiff identify the exact agreement and  provisions authorizing the balance, fees, interest, assignment and enforcement rights.”  Educational self-help material. Verify facts, court dates, and filings before use. No outcome is guaranteed.

CASE STUDIES  What the Cases Teach  Favorable and limiting examples keep the strategy sharp and honest.  09  Mitchell-James  Admission of using an unspecified original account did not concede account-specific debt-buyer  ownership. Lesson: admit the true history; fight the legal bridge.  Biehl  A bill of sale and statements were not enough where the account-level sale file link was missing.  Lesson: portfolio sale proof needs individual account connection.  Coleman  A bare affidavit claiming ownership without assignment documents was not enough at summary  judgment. Lesson: test conclusions against records.  Limiting principle  These are not Massachusetts guarantees. If plaintiff produces matched account-level evidence and a  competent witness, pivot to amount, terms, Article 9, consumer-law, and settlement terms.  Court phrasing  “The cases I reviewed teach why account-level transfer proof matters. I am asking for that same  basic connection in this case.”  Educational self-help material.
