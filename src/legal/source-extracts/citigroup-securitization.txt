
Citigroup securitizes its credit card debt by bundling together a large pool of credit card receivables and selling them to investors in the form of securities. The process of securitization involves several steps, which I'll outline below:
Geographic location. By pooling these accounts, Citigroup creates a diversified portfolio of credit card receivables, which helps to reduce the risk of default.
Structuring: Citigroup then structures the pool of credit card receivables into securities that are sold to investors. The securities are typically structured as tranches, with each tranche having a different level of risk and return.
Rating: The securities are then rated by credit rating agencies based on their credit quality. The highest-rated tranches are typically rated AAA, while the lower-rated tranches may be rated as low as BB.
Sale: The securities are then sold to investors such as pension funds, insurance companies, and hedge funds. The investors receive a regular stream of payments based on the cash flows generated by the underlying credit card receivables.
Servicing: Citigroup continues to service the credit card accounts even after they have been securitized. This involves collecting payments from cardholders, managing delinquencies, and servicing the accounts in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
